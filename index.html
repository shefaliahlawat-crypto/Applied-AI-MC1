Understanding What Is an AI Tool?

AI tools like ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude can write things for you in seconds. This page shows you what they are, how to try one safely, and how to check what they give you.

Before you continue

A few AI tools you can try

There are many AI tools. Three common ones are ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude. All three work in a simple way: you type what you want, and they write an answer for you.

  1. ChatGPT. Made by a company called OpenAI.
  2. Gemini. Made by Google.
  3. Claude. Made by a company called Anthropic.

How to get one on your phone or computer

  • On a computer. Open your browser, search for the tool's name, then sign in or make a free account.
  • On an Android phone. Open the Play Store, search for the tool's name, tap Install, then open the app and sign in.

Use only the tool your institution has approved, with your permitted account.

Your turn to reflect

The definition

It predicts likely language

At its core, an AI tool is a computer program. It has read a huge amount of text — books, articles and websites. From all that reading, it learned which words usually come after other words.

So when you type something, it does not search the internet. It does not think like a person. It looks at your words, then picks the next likely word, one at a time, until it has a full answer.

This is why its answers often sound smooth and sure of themselves. But it is only guessing. So the answer can still be wrong, even when it sounds correct.

The contrast

Not a search engine, not a calculator

A search engine finds pages that already exist. A calculator follows fixed rules to get one right answer. An AI tool writes brand-new text that sounds right for what you asked.

Three things it does well

Where I would use it without worry

  1. Drafting. A first version of a notice, email or checklist that you will then edit.
  2. Reshaping. Your rough points into clean sentences, or a long passage into short ones.
  3. Explaining. A difficult paragraph in simpler words, so you can get started on it.

Three things it does badly

Where I would not rely on it

  1. Facts and figures. It can give a wrong date, price or detail — and sound completely sure about it.
  2. Your local detail. Batch timings, submission dates, your syllabus, the tool list in your workshop.
  3. Big decisions. Safety steps, marks, money — anything you would put your name on.
