First contact

What This Tool Actually Is

You can hand an approved AI tool one small task and check what comes back. That is the whole skill on this page.

Before you read

Name one task you would let a computer draft for you, and one you would not.

One line is enough. I will come back to your answer once you have seen what this tool does well and badly.

A worked example

One small task, one quick check

Demonstration example — fictional

You need to remind your class group about tomorrow's workshop practical, so you type:

write a four-line reminder for tomorrow's workshop practical, ask everyone to bring their record book

A note comes back in seconds. You read it, correct the time, send it. The correcting mattered.

The definition

It predicts likely language

It has been trained on huge amounts of text and learned which words tend to follow which. Give it an instruction and it produces the language most likely to fit.

It is not looking anything up or working anything out. It assembles a likely answer, word by word. That is why the output reads well, and why it can read well and still be wrong.

The contrast

Not a search engine, not a calculator

A search engine finds pages that already exist. A calculator applies fixed rules. This tool composes fresh text that sounds right for your instruction.

Three things it does well

Where I would use it without hesitation

  1. Drafting. A first version of a notice, email or checklist that you will then edit.
  2. Reshaping. Your rough points into clean sentences, or a long passage into short ones.
  3. Explaining. A difficult paragraph in simpler words, so you can get started on it.

Three things it does badly

Where I would not rely on it

  1. Facts and figures. It can state a wrong date, price or specification with complete confidence.
  2. Your local detail. Batch timings, submission dates, your syllabus, the tool list in your workshop.
  3. Judgement with consequences. Safety steps, marks, money, anything you would put your name against.

Your turn

Take it for one real task

Try this

  • Pick one small task: a reminder for your class group, or a five-point checklist for a project submission.
  • Use your institution's approved tool and your permitted account.
  • Give it three things only: the task, the length, the tone. Leave out anything personal or confidential.

Five lines to keep

Recap

  1. It predicts likely text. It does not search and it does not calculate.
  2. It drafts, reshapes and explains well.
  3. It gets facts, local detail and consequential judgement wrong.
  4. Approved tool, permitted account, no personal or confidential data.
  5. Useful draft, human check, every time.
